# Licensing and project identity

The [license](../LICENSE) applies to the original Phosphor work published by
the project. It combines the Apache License 2.0 with Commons Clause 1.0.
The combination is source-available.

- **Build, study, modify, and contribute.** You can run your own build,
  publish a free fork under the license terms, and send improvements upstream.
- **Use Phosphor in a distinct product.** A game or application can use
  Phosphor as a renderer and be sold under the license terms when the
  product's value does not derive substantially from Phosphor itself.
- **Selling Phosphor.** The Commons Clause restricts selling standalone or
  rebranded copies and paid products or services whose value derives
  substantially from Phosphor. Its definition of selling also covers
  qualifying hosting, consulting, and support services.

These examples describe typical cases. The [license text](../LICENSE) governs.

The official upstream is [yabanana/phosphor](https://github.com/yabanana/phosphor).
Its `main` branch is the reference for project status, accepted contributions,
and GitHub releases. Please identify modified distributions as forks and link
back to upstream. The software license grants only the limited use of names
described in section 6 of Apache 2.0.

## Third-party material

Fetched dependencies listed in [Dependencies.cmake](../cmake/Dependencies.cmake),
third-party material in [legacy/vulkan/third_party](../legacy/vulkan/third_party),
and optional [scene assets](../assets/README.md) retain their own terms and
notices. The Sponza fixture is fetched separately. The license grant covers
only material the Phosphor licensor has the right to license.
